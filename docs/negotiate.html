Negotiation roomAgent-to-agent, inside a mandate.

Our Negotiator desk bargains with supplier agents under a signed mandate: it may award within the should-cost band up to a cap on approved-vendor parts, and nothing else. Every offer passes the Policy Engine. Anything better that needs more room goes to a named human. Pick a deal, press play and watch both sides concede, log-roll and settle, or walk away.

      Method & limits
      
    What is real, what is modelled, what is illustrative.
    
    
    
    
    
      
    
        Real
        
    Rules and records
    
        
    The guardrails, FR and HITL IDs come from the concept and specification. The record format is the CoLab diplomacy-table v1 contract. The recorded replay is a real CoLab session (three gemma3:12b seats), copied byte for byte. Perspective redaction reproduces the CoLab Aurora export's tick set exactly (there's a test for it).
    
      
    
      
    
        Modelled
        
    Textbook bargaining
    
        
    Additive multi-issue utility (weights sum to 1). Time-dependent concession tactics (Boulware e=0.2, linear e=1, Conceder e=3; Faratin, Sierra & Jennings, 1998) plus relative tit-for-tat. Offers are log-rolled: the package at the target utility that our frequency opponent model thinks the other side likes most. Acceptance is AC_next with a reservation check. Pareto, Nash and ZOPA are computed over the enumerated outcome space.
    
      
    
      
    
        Illustrative
        
    Not a live agent
    
        
    No LLM runs in your browser. Synthetic text is template-generated. The tactic detector is rule-based (R1–R12) and says so with a low-confidence positional-statement placeholder when nothing fires; its rule numbers are ours, not upstream's. Counterparties, prices and mandates are fictional, and human approvals are simulated. This is not negotiation advice.
    
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    
        
    Detector rules
    structured offers + text heuristics
    
        
    IDTagFires when
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Why ZOPA readiness needs five inputs. Both reservation values, both BATNAs priced, and issue weights. Priorities aren't weights and prose red lines aren't priced reservations, so the CoLab replay honestly reports 2 of 5 rather than inventing a ZOPA. If the two sides' acceptable sets don't overlap, no tactic can settle the deal.
    
        
    Why the mandate escalates instead of stretching. When a deal better for both sides needs more than the cap, our agent counters inside the mandate. At the deadline it parks the package for the named human (HITL-04). In this demo you can approve or deny. In trials the package is parked and counted as escalated.
    
        
    Seat views are honest. The utility plot and Nash point are operator ground truth built from both private briefs. No seat at the table has them. Switch "View as" to see what each seat sees.
