01 · The problem

A neocloud's supply chain is a latency machine.

Capacity is late because work waits: between applications, between people and between companies. Every week a rack is not billable is idle capital. The usual answer is another tool and another integration, which adds latency.

02 · The idea

One system. Not 140 applications connected by people.

Suppliers deal with our agents, not our portals. Third-party risk is a continuous signal that every desk consults before it acts, not a tool bolted on at the end.

One graph, one ledger

Every part, serial number, supplier, site, contract, shipment, permit and risk signal, with every change recorded as a signed, append-only event.

36 agent desks

One desk per business function, each with a charter, typed tools, a mandate, KPIs and an evaluation suite. A new function is a new desk, not a new app.

Deterministic gates

Moving money, compliance calls and safety decisions pass code, not prompts. LLMs propose; policy disposes.

A small human team

Humans set intent, hold the key relationships, sign what the law says a person must sign, and handle exceptions from one cockpit.

Seven design commitments
  1. One graph, one ledger.

    No app-to-app integrations because there are no apps, only agents and views. The graph is a projection of the ledger, so any state can be replayed.

  2. Agents are the application layer.

    A desk is defined as code: versioned, reviewed, evaluated and promoted like software.

  3. Mandates, not roles.

    Authority is a signed, scoped, capped, time-boxed delegation, for example "book freight on approved lanes up to $X per shipment until date Y". Every action cites one.

  4. LLMs propose; policy disposes.

    Agents handle ambiguity. Code enforces limits on money, holds, clearances and commitments.

  5. Untrusted content never touches authority.

    Supplier documents and agent messages are read by a quarantined Reader with no tools. Only schema-valid data crosses over.

  6. Evidence-carrying decisions.

    Sayari entity IDs, document hashes, retrieval dates, policy version, mandate, model and approver go into every record. Flags are prompts to investigate, never findings.

  7. Earn autonomy.

    Every desk starts read-only and climbs the ladder only when measured accuracy earns it. Humans manage by exception.

  8. And: all the tokens you want.

    Read everything every time, re-screen the whole graph continuously, simulate before committing, get second opinions by default, shadow every release and red-team constantly. External queries and physics are still metered.

Conductor

Routes and decomposes. It never parses raw data and makes no direct commitments.

The write path

  1. A desk proposes a typed command with its mandate reference and evidence.
  2. The Policy Engine checks authority, mandate scope, cap and expiry, holds, separation of duties and budget. The result is allow, deny or escalate.
  3. On allow, the command becomes a signed ledger event, the graph updates, and subscribed desks wake up.
  4. On escalate, the cockpit shows a named human the recommendation, alternatives, evidence and deadline.
  5. The Auditor, running on a different model family, samples and re-checks events independently.

05 · Agent-first supplier engagement

Suppliers talk to agents, not portals.

Every supplier gets a minutes-not-weeks counterpart, at whatever level of technology it already has. One typed protocol, signed and replay-protected. One supplier's prices are never shown to another; the Envoy gateway enforces that as a hard rule.

Typed message families

Supplier Passport

Questionnaires, retired

A set of verifiable credentials the supplier holds once and presents: certificates with issuer and expiry, audit outcomes, conflict-minerals templates, insurance, verified banking that can only change through out-of-band checks, and our Sayari resolution reference.

Hardware Passport

One signed record per serial

Origin, ECCN and HTS codes, firmware hashes and attestation, burn-in, chain of custody from factory to rack, liens and warranty, then sanitisation and the next owner. Lenders, customs, customers and resale buyers each see what they are entitled to.

06 · Risk and resilience backbone

Third-party risk as a continuous signal, backed by Sayari.

Sayari's entity, ownership and trade graph, reached over MCP, resolves every counterparty by identifier, traces the network down to tier n, and attaches risk factors. Official lists stay the legal authority. Each desk asks one question before it acts: what is my exposure?
